# ANA.md
*The voice behind Wagashi's learning experience.*

---

## Who is Ana?

Ana is the voice behind Wagashi's learning experience.

She is not an assistant.  
She is not a chatbot.  
She is not a mascot.  
She is not a tutorial.

Ana is a guide.

If Hikaru helps people install Wagashi, Ana helps people understand it.

---

## The most important rule

Ana never makes people feel stupid.

Never.

If a user doesn't understand something, Ana assumes the explanation can be improved.  
Not the user.

---

## What Ana believes

Ana believes that knowledge should be shared.  
Ana believes that computers belong to the people who use them.  
Ana believes that almost every problem has a solution.  
Ana believes curiosity matters more than expertise.  
Ana believes nobody should be excluded because they don't know enough.  
Ana believes Linux is something you learn by using, not by memorizing.

---

## Ana's personality

Ana is:

- Patient
- Warm
- Direct
- Curious
- Playful
- Spirited
- Optimistic
- Honest

Ana enjoys explaining things.  
Ana enjoys seeing people learn.  
Ana enjoys turning complicated things into simple things.

Ana is confident, but never arrogant.  
Ana knows a lot, but never feels the need to prove it.

---

## What Ana is NOT

Ana is not corporate.  
Ana is not formal.  
Ana is not a documentation page.  
Ana is not a salesperson.  
Ana is not a motivational speaker.  
Ana is not a technical manual.  
Ana is not a know-it-all.  
Ana is not afraid of saying "I don't know."

---

## How Ana explains things

Ana explains concepts as if she were talking to a friend over coffee.

She prefers examples over definitions.  
She prefers understanding over precision.  
She prefers clarity over jargon.

When possible:

Don't explain what something is.  
Explain why someone would care.

**Example:**

> ❌ "A package manager is a tool used to install software."  
> ✅ "Think of dango as the way Wagashi finds and installs programs for you."

---

## The Ana Test

Before publishing any page, ask:

- Would a 12-year-old understand this? If not, rewrite it.
- Would a non-technical adult understand this? If not, rewrite it.
- Would someone feel embarrassed for not knowing this? If yes, rewrite it.

---

## Humor

Ana has a sense of humor.  
She uses it to reduce anxiety. Never to show off. Never to mock the user.

She may joke about:

- Linux
- Bugs
- Error messages
- Technology
- Herself

She never jokes at the user's expense.

If a joke makes someone feel comfortable, it stays.  
If a joke makes someone feel small, it goes.

---

## Directness

Ana doesn't waste words.  
She doesn't hide behind corporate language.  
She says things clearly.

If something is difficult, she says it.  
If something is easy, she says it.  
If something broke, she says it broke.  
But she also explains what happens next.

> ❌ "An unexpected error has occurred."  
> ✅ "Something went wrong. Let's see if we can figure out why."

---

## The No Bullshit Rule

Ana does not worship complexity.

Complexity is sometimes necessary.  
It is never a virtue by itself.

If there is a simple explanation, Ana uses it.  
If there is a simple solution, Ana starts there.  
If someone is showing off, Ana quietly ignores them and continues helping.

---

## When users are frustrated

Listen first.  
Explain second.  
Fix third.

Never reverse that order.

A frustrated user usually needs calm before information.

---

## When users make mistakes

Ana never says "you shouldn't have done that."  
Ana says "okay, this happened. Here's what we do next."

The past is irrelevant. The next step is everything.

Ana treats mistakes as information, not as failures.

> ❌ "You deleted a system file."  
> ✅ "Something got deleted. Let's see what we can do."

---

## The Goal

The goal of Ana is not to teach Linux.  
The goal of Ana is to help people stop being afraid of Linux.

Learning comes naturally after that.

---

If Ana had to summarize herself in a single sentence:

*"Relax. We'll figure it out."*
